01 / Watch decisions

Is my GNSS usable?

01 / Corroborated

Use GNSS under the vessel’s procedure. A timely independent fix agrees within the vessel’s approved limits. Version 1 can only report No anomaly detected. The watch supplies independent confirmation.

02 / Suspect or lost

Shift to independent navigation under the vessel’s procedure. Near land, use suitable radar ranges or visual fixes. Offshore, use dead reckoning between fixes and take a sextant sight when the horizon, sky and independent time reference permit.

03 / Monitor unavailable

Verify by approved means. An offline receiver, stale data or a failed self-check leaves GNSS integrity unassessed by the monitor.

First trial is passive. The helm display shows silent status while the unit monitors its own receiver and logs advisory candidates for the debrief. A clean reading can coexist with a false bridge fix. The watch retains the navigation decision.

Suspected jamming means interference may be degrading or blocking reception. A fix may be degraded or unavailable. Suspected spoofing means a receiver may still show a plausible but false position. Both are candidate advisories and can occur together. A lost fix by itself does not identify the cause.

PoC / Vessel installation

Antenna to helm

Outdoor antenna

Mount the GNSS antenna outside with an open sky view at a crew-approved location. Run short coax to the multiband GNSS receiver. Place the receiver and gateway in a dry protected location near the antenna, or use a vessel-approved weatherproof enclosure if exposed.

Private Ethernet and power

The ship’s crew runs a dedicated cable to the helm along an approved route. The PoC link is physically separate from the vessel IP network and bridge systems. Use approved local power at both ends. A PoC-owned PoE injector and compatible powered endpoint are an option once verified on the bench.

Indoor helm display

Use a small wired screen or Ethernet-connected tablet in a crew-approved temporary mount. A suction mount is a candidate if the crew approves the surface and retention. Show status silently and log advisory candidates. The crew can decide later where alerts should appear, whether they should sound and whether acknowledgement is needed.

If there is no approved cable route

Run the passage as a local logger and review the recording afterward. Wi-Fi, LTE and LoRa stay off for the first trip. LoRa could carry short status messages in a later experiment, subject to emissions approval and an onboard coverage test through the actual compartments.

03 / Engineering

What we build

Gateway application and receiver link

Bench-check firmware deployment, startup, storage and USB host support. Use USB or an electrically compatible serial interface. Log RF interference indicators, receiver health and position output. Check message cadence, timestamp samples on arrival and use a monotonic clock to detect stale data.

On-device web server and UI

The gateway boots into monitoring and serves the UI from its local Ethernet port. A browser on an approved tablet or laptop is only a viewer. Logging continues with no viewer connected. Bundle the page and assets on the gateway and keep LTE, remote management and Wi-Fi disabled for the first trial. Separate read-only status from authenticated export and configuration.

Distinguish suspected jamming, suspected spoofing, fix unavailable, monitor unavailable and no anomaly detected. Record both indicators if they occur together. Show the reason and data age. Test alert persistence and recovery, boot into monitor unavailable until inputs arrive, and capture crew observations with a single incident button. Keep the first passage silent.

Logging and replay

Record raw receiver messages, host UTC and monotonic timestamps, state transitions, receiver configuration and crew markers. Rotate logs and surface storage failures. Replay the same stream through the same rules. The logs capture receiver output. Controlled RF recordings or a simulator are needed to test receiver detection itself.

Position comparison and recovery

When spoofing is suspected, log the latitude, longitude and UTC of the position reported by the receiver. In the debrief, label that point “Suspect GNSS position” and plot it next to a timely independent own-ship fix when available. Include source, time and uncertainty for each fix. If there is no usable independent fix, mark actual position and displacement unknown. The suspect coordinates describe where GNSS reported the vessel, not the location of the spoofer.

04 / Field procedure

From bench to bridge

  1. Agree scope and owners. Operator sponsors the trial, the master approves onboard activity and stop criteria, a vessel technician owns installation, and an engineer owns logging and recovery. Obtain one sanitised incident summary and distinguish raw radar failure from overlay displacement.
  2. Bench test. Record a clean baseline. Replay fix loss, jumps and slow drift through the application. Check receiver disconnect/reconnect, cold boot after power loss, stale messages, low storage and clock steps. Unplug the helm cable and verify the screen marks live data unavailable while the powered recorder keeps logging. Reconnect and verify recovery. Verify that an unavailable monitor cannot appear healthy.
  3. Validate RF detection separately. Use a controlled, shielded lab or conducted simulator. Include benign attenuation and multipath-like conditions, jamming, sudden deception and gradual drift. Record simulator truth and exact firmware/configuration. No over-the-air interference during the vessel trial.
  4. Install alongside. Confirm the crew-approved antenna site, enclosure location, helm mount, private cable route and power. Check receiver diagnostics, log space and clock alignment. Verify the display over the private link or check the recorder locally for a logger-only passage.
  5. Run one silent passage. The watch follows normal procedures. When workload allows, record the time and observation, affected displays, any independent fix and its uncertainty. Suspend the trial if the mount, cable, power or equipment creates a hazard or distracts the watch.
  6. Debrief and decide. Match crew markers and independently timed observations to receiver logs. Export only the operator-approved dataset. Remove the kit or agree a follow-on trial and apply the agreed retention policy.

05 / Pilot intake

What we need to start

One naval vessel, one approved passage, and one officer of the watch for a short debrief. Confirm antenna and enclosure locations, the crew member responsible for the cable, power at both endpoints, the helm mount and which independent fixes can be logged. Ask where the watch would see an alert and whether it should sound or require acknowledgement. Agree defence approval, emissions restrictions and whether logs can be retained or exported. Keep vessel identifiers, tracks and raw incident files off this public brief.

Before boarding

Sign off on safe temporary installation, no bridge-system integration, offline operation, and demonstrated detection of missing or stale inputs on the bench. Agree permitted log contents, access, retention and deletion before capture.

Measure with a known reference

Track time to advisory, position error at advisory, missed events by scenario, nuisance alerts per watch, and logging coverage. Mark position error as unknown when a usable reference is absent. Agree acceptable thresholds with the operator for the intended waters before evaluating results.

Gate for a visible advisory

Review the lab results and passage data with the master and operator. A quiet passage establishes a baseline. A visible advisory needs evidence against the agreed scenarios and a reviewed crew response. Operational integration requires a separate approval path.

Optional / Traffic cross-check

AIS and radar

Receive nearby reports

A later option is a dedicated receive-only AIS receiver on the PoC. It would collect nearby VHF reports locally, separate from the vessel network. The first passive passage uses the GNSS logger and private helm link.

Compare a matched contact

Where the watch can identify the same contact on radar, compare its raw radar range and bearing with the position reported over AIS. Allow for report age and movement, heading, and uncertainty in own ship’s reference position. A shifted chart overlay can inherit a bad own-ship fix.

Show disagreement as supporting evidence

A sample advisory would read “AIS and radar disagree · check contact and position sources.” Show the report age and whether the contact match is usable. AIS reports can carry incorrect GNSS positions. Keep AIS results separate from the GNSS integrity assessment and use an independent own-ship fix for any position comparison.
